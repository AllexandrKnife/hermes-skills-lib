# Sun Tzu Strategic Assessment of the Russia-Ukraine War

Diagnostic framework applying principles from "The Art of War" to analyze Russia's strategy in the Russia-Ukraine war (2022-2026).

**Core insight from session 6 July 2026:** Sun Tzu's framework diagnoses non-optimal strategy with overwhelming resource superiority. Russia's strategy is suboptimal but not necessarily losing — resource asymmetry compensates for strategic inefficiency.

## 1. The War Should Not Have Started
**Principle:** "He who does not calculate risks should not begin a war"
**Reality:** Russia invaded without securing Ukraine's diplomatic neutrality, NATO fragmentation, Western passivity, or speed of victory.

**Caveat:** Even a poorly-planned war can be won if resource disparity is large enough.

## 2. Economic War Instead of Positional War
**Principle:** "The best war is to destroy the enemy's plans. The worst is to besiege cities"
**Reality:** Russia fights for cities — Sun Tzu's lowest-priority approach.

What Sun Tzu would do differently: total port blockade, rail network dismantling, grain chain destruction, energy system paralysis for years.

**Irony:** Ukraine's Middle Strike Campaign IS Sun Tzu in practice.

## 3. Divide the West, Don't Unite It
**Principle:** "Do not let the enemy unite"
**Reality:** One of Russia's greatest strategic failures — unified Europe and US against it.

## 4. Strike the Weak, Not the Strong
**Principle:** "Water avoids heights and flows downward — the army avoids the strong and strikes the weak"

## 5. Create Unbearable Costs for the West
**Principle:** "He who knows war makes the enemy follow him, not the other way around"

## 6. Speed Over Protraction
**Principle:** "War values victory, not duration. Protracted war benefits no one"

## 7. Leave the Enemy an Escape Route
**Principle:** "When surrounding the enemy, leave him an exit"

## Diagnostic Summary (Corrected)

Sun Tzu would say:
*"You fight suboptimally — but you have overwhelming resources. Your problem is not that you're losing — it's that you're paying too much for what you could have achieved cheaper."*

## Key Analytical Nuance (added July 2026)

The original assessment contained a bias: assuming "Russia is losing / trapped" because its strategy is suboptimal. **This is incorrect.** Sun Tzu criticizes inefficiency, not outcomes. Resource asymmetry compensates:

| Factor | Ukraine | Russia |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Population loss | ~2.4M (6-7%) | Manageable |
| Economy | 100% dependent on West | Self-sufficient |
| Weapons | 100% imported (except drones) | Domestic production |
| Territory | Losing | Slowly gaining |
| Mobilization | Total (up to age 60) | One partial in 2022 |

**Bottom line:** A war can be won with suboptimal strategy if resource disparity is large enough. Russia is not losing this war — and likely will not lose it unless the West fundamentally changes the balance of power.
