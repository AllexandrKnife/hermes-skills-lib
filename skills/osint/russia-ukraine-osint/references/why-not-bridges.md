# Why Russia Doesn't Destroy the Bridges Across the Dnieper

Analysis of a recurring strategic question: if Russia has long-range strike capability, why not systematically destroy the bridges across the Dnieper River to isolate Ukraine's right bank?

## Seven Hypotheses

### 1. Russia Will Need Them for Its Own Offensive
If Russia's strategic goal includes crossing to the right bank, intact bridges are essential for heavy equipment logistics. Destroy now = rebuild later under fire. Crimea Bridge is the cautionary example: damaged three times, months of repairs each time.

### 2. Cost Asymmetry (1 Bridge = 1,000 Gas Stations)
Dnieper bridges are massive reinforced concrete structures. A Geran drone (20kg warhead) can't punch a span. Requires heavy missiles (Kalibr, Kh-101, Iskander) at $2-6M each. One bridge = 10-20 missiles ($20-120M). Same budget = 1,000-6,000 Gerans hitting gas stations and fuel trucks — thousands of tactical disruptions vs one strategic inconvenience.

### 3. Ukraine Has Already Adapted
After the Kakhovka dam destruction and Kherson bridge losses (2022), Ukraine deployed:
- Pontoon bridges and ferry crossings
- Decentralized logistics (many small crossings instead of one major one)
- Disruption from a bridge hit now = days, not weeks

### 4. Attrition Strategy, Not Decapitation
Russian doctrine is attrition warfare. The goal is not one decisive blow but thousands of small cuts — exhausting logistics over months and years. Hitting gas stations, fuel trucks, railways achieves systemic attrition. Hitting one bridge achieves a 2-week disruption.

### 5. Political Escalation Cost
Dnieper bridges are civilian infrastructure critical to Ukraine's economy and humanitarian situation. Total bridge destruction would be an escalation threshold with unpredictable Western response.

### 6. Internal Bureaucratic Conflict
VKS (air force) lobbies for strategic targets (bridges, ports, energy). Drone operators and ground forces push for tactical targets (gas stations, convoys, fuel trucks). The second group wins because:
- Drones are cheaper and more available than missiles
- Results are immediate (burning station = no fuel for a battalion)
- No VKS coordination required

### 7. Strategic Priority Is Crimea, Not the Dnieper
Russia's real strategic priority is preventing Crimea's isolation. Ukraine has almost achieved this via the Middle Strike Campaign (R-280 highway). Russia's asymmetric response is not defending Crimea directly but attacking Ukrainian gas stations to hinder Ukraine's southern offensive capability.

## Conclusion

Russia chose "a thousand cuts" over "one amputation" not from lack of capability but from:
1. Economic asymmetry (1 bridge = 1000 gas stations)
2. Ukrainian adaptation (bridges replaced by crossings)
3. Political escalation costs
4. Future operational needs (bridges needed for any right-bank offensive)
5. Institutional conflict (VKS vs drone operators)

The internal debate (Онуфриенко vs Два майора/Поддубный) reflects this unresolved strategic tension within Russian military-analytic community.
